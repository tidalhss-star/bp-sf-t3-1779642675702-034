### 질문 사다리 (Question Ladder)
What exactly makes a BARS anchor valid compared to a standard KPI?
Most people assume the scale itself matters more than the examples written on it, but I've seen anchors that define 'good' behavior like they define 'working hours'.

### 현장 단서 (Field Clues)
The anchors often look like a contract clause where one side gives up without realizing it.
For instance, an anchor describing 'high performance' shouldn't just say 'sales increase', but include specific behavioral steps like 'initiated 3 client meetings'.
Mapo Shirt Room knows that a suit doesn't fit if the shoulder is too tight; similarly, evaluation forms don't work if the criteria are vague.
A good example feels like a tailored jacket, not an off-the-rack shirt, which is why I always check for version 2024 standards in my notes.

### 판단 메모 (Judgment Memo)
If your goal is to make employees understand what's expected without feeling controlled, the anchor must describe actions they can observe and verify.
Think of it like checking a hanger tag; you see the size clearly before buying so there's no dispute later when measuring yourself.
When I review their forms, I look for three things: Is the behavior specific? Can it be measured by anyone else? Does it match the actual job content they do daily?

Ultimately, remember that evaluation isn't about punishing mistakes but guiding how to fit into the company culture properly.
Just as a shirt room owner adjusts sleeves silently so you don't notice until the cut is perfect, HR should tweak anchors without making noise.
If you follow this approach, your employees will feel supported rather than watched when they open their evaluation apps next month.
